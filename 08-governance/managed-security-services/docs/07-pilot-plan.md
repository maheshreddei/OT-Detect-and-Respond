# 07 — Pilot (Lighthouse) Plan

The single most important step. A pilot converts a slide deck into demonstrated capability, surfaces the real operational gaps, and gives sales a reference/case study. It's also the decision gate that de-risks the big spend.

## Pilot options
- **Internal lab / testbed** (fastest, lowest risk) — build a small OT lab (a PLC or two, an HMI, a historian, an NDR sensor) and demonstrate the full chain: visibility → detection → response. Great for enablement and demos.
- **Friendly client** (strongest proof) — a low-risk, co-managed pilot with an existing client that has OT exposure. Produces a real case study and a potential first paying account.
- Ideally do both: lab first to build/enable, then a friendly-client pilot to prove market fit.

## Scope (keep it tight)
- One site/segment or the lab; passive NDR + Tier-1 log onboarding.
- A focused detection set: **IT/OT boundary crossing, remote-access abuse, and a few protocol write/command detections** — the highest-value, fastest wins.
- One or two IR playbooks tabletop-tested.

## Success criteria (define before you start — this is the gate)
- Platform deployed passively with **no operational disruption**.
- Asset visibility achieved; baseline of normal established.
- A set of **working, validated detections** demonstrated against test cases.
- End-to-end **detection → triage → response** shown.
- Stakeholder sign-off (and, for a client pilot, a reference/testimonial).
- Internal enablement: the nucleus can run it.

## Exit gate
Meet the criteria → **go** to Launch (Phase 3) with proven capability, a case study, and a trained team. Miss them → adjust scope/skills/platform and re-run, or stop — with only the small pilot spend at risk.

## Why the pilot wins the initiative
It turns "trust me" into "here's it working." Leadership funds proven things; sales sells demonstrated things; clients buy referenced things. The pilot delivers all three at a fraction of the full investment.
